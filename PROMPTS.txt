THE INVOICE CHASER — the three prompts, exactly as used in the episode.
Paste them into your AI assistant in order. Prompt 1 produces the first (broken) build.
Prompt 2 and Prompt 3 are the corrections that turn it into the finished tool.

================================ PROMPT 1 ================================
Build me an invoice chaser as ONE html file I can open in a browser with the internet
off. I will paste in two CSV lists my bookkeeping already keeps:

invoices.csv — invoice, client, email, issued, due, amount, status
payments.csv — date, invoice, client, amount

Show me the total I'm owed, an aging table (not yet due, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ days),
and the list of who is late, in what order. For each late client, draft the reminder
email for me — gentle if they're a little late, firm if it's been a while, a final
notice if it's been very long — ready to open in my own mail app. Nothing uploads
anywhere; the page must work offline.

================================ PROMPT 2 ================================
The first build hid most of my overdue money. Four corrections, and they are rules,
not suggestions:

- A payment binds to an INVOICE NUMBER, never to a client name. A client paying one
  invoice says nothing about their other invoices.
- An invoice's balance is computed: amount billed minus payments received against that
  invoice id. The status column is never used for arithmetic — treat it as a rumor.
- Parse the CSV properly: a quoted field may contain commas ("2,450.00" is an amount,
  not two fields).
- Any row you cannot read — an amount or a date that isn't one, a duplicate invoice id,
  a payment pointing at an invoice that doesn't exist — is REFUSED out loud, listed
  with its row number and the reason, never guessed at and never dropped in silence.

================================ PROMPT 3 ================================
Now make it impossible for this page to lie to me:

- All money is carried as whole cents, with one written rounding rule, and rendered by
  one formatter.
- The books' as-of date is typed into a settings box. Never read the machine clock:
  the same books must produce the same report on any day they are opened.
- Every overdue invoice belongs to exactly ONE aging bucket (1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ —
  day 30 is 1–30, day 31 is 31–60), and the five aging rows must re-total to the
  outstanding figure to the cent before anything renders.
- An overpayment is a CREDIT: flag it, show what I owe them back, and never write a
  reminder for it.
- A status that says "paid" while money is still owed is flagged as a disagreement, and
  the invoice stays in the chase.
- Audit every run: every dollar figure inside every drafted email must trace back to
  the ledger, every overdue invoice must appear in exactly one email, and no settled
  invoice may be named in any email. If one check fails, withhold ALL the emails and
  print DO NOT TRUST THESE REMINDERS with the reason.
- Settings I can change, and each must visibly change the output: as-of date, grace
  days, the two tone thresholds, my business name, my reply-to address, and the wording
  of all three reminder templates.
